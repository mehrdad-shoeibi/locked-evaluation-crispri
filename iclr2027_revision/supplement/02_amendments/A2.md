## 0. Why the timing is the point

Sessions L10b, L11, and L11.1 each printed `EXTERNAL_METRIC_ON_HYPOTHESIS: NO` and
`MODEL_FIT_EXECUTED: NO` for Phase 4. No external transfer number exists. This amendment
is therefore made blind to every quantity it governs.

It is committed **before** L11.2 runs, deliberately. L11.2 will report whether RF's
validation degeneracy on `m̃` is a ranking failure or a calibration failure. If this
amendment were written after that number, the commitment below would be conditioned on
it. Written first, it cannot be.

## 1. The primary external head does not change. Ever.

Phase 1 froze its head selection per arm before any test number existed: `z` → RF,
`[z; m̃]` → RF, C1 → HistGB, C2 → RF, C3 → HistGB, C4 → RF, C5 → RF. Lock §22 forbids a
per-screen head choice, §11 requires the freeze to precede any test number, and §28
forbids a later phase revising an earlier scientific choice.

**Phase 4 inherits that frozen selection as the primary analysis, unconditionally.**

This holds even though L11 established — at a narrow scope: the App. F RF configuration
(`n_estimators=200, max_depth=20, min_samples_leaf=2`), on this `m̃` block, on the VCC
validation split, under this protocol — that RF's in-support validation MSE (0.638)
exceeds validation `var(y)` (0.402), while a linear head on the identical `m̃` reaches
+0.259.

Two reasons, and the second matters more than the first:

1. Lock §28 prohibits the revision outright.
2. Phase 1's test values have already been seen. A change made now — even on grounds
   that are themselves validation-only, and therefore legitimate as a *criterion* —
   would be a change made with test knowledge in the room. Reviewers cannot audit our
   internal state. What they can audit is that the primary was frozen and stayed frozen.

## 2. A linear secondary is pre-registered now

For every external arm containing `m̃`, a **linear head** is added as a **secondary**
analysis, reported alongside the frozen primary. It replaces nothing.

- If primary and secondary agree, the external result is robust to the head, and the
  paper says so.
- If they disagree, **that disagreement is the finding** and is reported as such. It is
  not resolved by presenting whichever looks better.

This is added unconditionally rather than contingent on L11.2's outcome, so that no
reader need wonder whether the condition was chosen after the fact. It is a sensitivity
analysis on one head, in an appendix — not a new result, and not a revision of anything
frozen. The *exactly one new result* constraint is unaffected: the one new
result remains the Geneformer arm.

### 2.1 Secondary linear-head implementation — locked now, no freedom left

For every external arm containing `m̃`, the secondary head is ordinary
`sklearn.linear_model.LinearRegression` with `fit_intercept=True` and otherwise default
settings. It is fit **once** on the identical VCC-train rows and the identical
preprocessed feature matrix used by that arm, using the frozen VCC-train `m̃` scaler
(§6B). There is **no** hyperparameter search, no validation-based family selection, no
target-side calibration, and no screen-specific refit. The fitted VCC model is applied
zero-shot to RPE1 and K562-essential exactly as the primary head is.

`LinearRegression` rather than Ridge is chosen for consistency with the manuscript's own
MAG-LINEAR arm, which L10b established is `magnitude_only_LR` — sklearn
`LinearRegression`, not Ridge (`phase11_strong_baselines/run.py:339`).

**If the Lock already specifies a different exact linear estimator for these arms, the
Lock wins.** Quote it before execution and STOP on any inconsistency rather than
choosing.

### 2.2 Scope of the secondary — and what it may not be used to claim

The secondary applies to arms **containing `m̃`**, and not to `z`-only arms. This is
deliberate, not an oversight: unregularised OLS on a 768-dimensional embedding is a
near-degenerate estimator — Phase 1 already found Ridge (α = 1.0) "deeply negative on
all arms" — so a linear head there would produce an uninformative number rather than a
head-sensitivity check.

**Consequence, stated so it cannot be glossed later.** Because the secondary does not
cover `z`-only arms, it **cannot** be used to re-evaluate H-FM3 (`[z; m̃]` versus `z`),
which requires both members under a common head. The secondary is reported strictly as
a **head-sensitivity analysis on the `m̃`-containing arms** — it answers "does this arm's
external result depend on the head?" and nothing more. No pre-registered hypothesis is
re-tested under it.

## 3. A cell-count sensitivity is pre-registered now

L11.1 established that K562-essential's log1p-CP10K deltas are genuinely smaller than
VCC's and RPE1's larger (raw `‖x‖₂` medians 2.20 / 3.12 / 4.37), and that this is **not**
explained by zero-fill — raw `‖x‖₂` and `max|x|` are zero-fill-immune and were identical
under a mapped-only recompute, yet remain depressed.

Magnitude statistics are inflated by estimation noise, and the inflation scales with
cells per construct: for `x̂ = x + ε` with `var(ε) ∝ 1/n`, `E[‖x̂‖₂²] = ‖x‖₂² + d·σ²/n`.
The manuscript controls sample size for the **endpoint** (§6.3, 27/788 subsamples); it
is not established that the same control applies to the **features**.

**Pre-registered sensitivity:** compare the four magnitude components across the three
screens restricted to constructs within a **common cell-count band**, and report whether
the K562 < VCC < RPE1 ordering survives at matched cell counts.

### 3.1 Matched-band rule — locked before any magnitude value is read

The band is determined from **cell counts only**, mechanically, before any magnitude
statistic is inspected:

> `lower = max(Q25_VCC, Q25_RPE1, Q25_K562)`
> `upper = min(Q75_VCC, Q75_RPE1, Q75_K562)`
>
> using empirical quartiles over each screen's eligible construct set. Retain every
> construct whose cell count lies in `[lower, upper]`.

**This interval is not adjusted after inspecting magnitude values, under any
circumstance.** If `lower ≥ upper`, report `MATCHED_BAND_ADEQUATE = NO` and do not widen
the band in that session — an empty overlap is itself the finding, and it means the
screens cannot be compared on magnitude without matching.

**Adequacy is judged by uncertainty, not by an invented minimum n.** Report the surviving
construct count per screen **and a bootstrap CI on each matched-band median**. If the
CIs overlap, the comparison is inconclusive at the available sample size and is reported
as inconclusive — no count threshold is imposed, because any such threshold would be an
arbitrary number introduced into a pre-registration.

Stated in advance so the interpretation cannot drift after the numbers land:

- Ordering **survives** within the pre-registered common cell-count band → the
  cross-screen magnitude ordering is **robust to this cell-count-restricted
  sensitivity** and is not explained by gross differences in cell-count support.
  **This does not rule cell count out as a contributor**, and no such claim may be made:
  `[lower, upper]` restricts to a common band, it does not match distributions within
  it. The within-band cell-count distributions must be reported per screen so the
  strength of the claim is visible as a number rather than asserted.
- Ordering **collapses or reverses** → estimation noise is doing substantial work, and
  the manuscript must disclose that its magnitude features — unlike its endpoint — are
  not size-matched across screens.
- Cell-count distributions **do not overlap** enough to support the comparison → that is
  itself the answer: the screens cannot be compared on magnitude without matching, and
  the paper must say so.

A within-screen correlation between cell count and magnitude may support any of these
but **attributes none of them**. No causal or quantitative attribution of the
between-screen shift may be claimed from correlation alone.

## 4. A correction to my own earlier reasoning, recorded

The reconciliation memo §4 downgraded the C1 estimation-noise concern on the grounds
that what matters is `z` and `x` being estimated from the same cell sets. That is
correct for the FM-versus-expression comparison and remains so. **It was generalised too
far.** It does not cover cross-screen comparability of the magnitude features
themselves — which is the quantity the paper's headline claim is about. §3 above is the
repair. The original downgrade stands within its proper scope.
