## 0. What L11.2 found, and why it needs an amendment rather than a fix

Cells per construct differ sharply across the three screens, and the distributions are
**disjoint at the interquartile level**:

| screen | median | IQR | min | max | n |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| VCC | 26 | 18–40 | 10 | 121 | 5,740 |
| RPE1 | 73 | 48–113 | 25 | 3,451 | 1,932 |
| K562-essential | 118 | 79–174 | 27 | 1,996 | 1,903 |

Within every screen, fewer cells are associated with larger **measured** magnitude
(Spearman down to −0.72 on VCC). This pattern is **consistent with** the expected
finite-sample inflation

    E[‖x̂‖₂² | x] = ‖x‖₂² + d·σ²/n

but the cross-construct association **does not by itself identify estimation noise as
the cause.** True biological effect, viability, and cell recovery may co-vary with `n`:
a construct whose knockdown kills cells yields fewer cells *and* may have a larger true
effect, producing the same negative association with no statistical inflation at all.

Both mechanisms predict the observed sign. Separating sensitivity to sampling depth from
those biological possibilities is precisely what this amendment exists to do — so it must
not assume the answer in its own premise.

The features are **not** size-matched (`loader.py:403`, `x_cell_accumulator.py:178` —
means over all group cells; `min_cells = 10` is a floor, not matching), while the
**endpoint is** size-controlled (§6.3, 27/788 subsamples). That asymmetry is the finding.

The A2 §3.1 matched band is **empty** (`lower = 79 > upper = 40`), so band restriction
cannot answer the question. Actual subsampling is required.

## 2. The matched sensitivity — fully specified now

**Two `n` values, both mechanically derived from cell counts alone. No arbitrary
choice.**

- `n_match = 10` — the feature path's existing floor (`min_cells = 10`). Every construct
  in every screen qualifies (minima 10 / 25 / 27, all ≥ 10); **full retention**.
- `n_match = 27` — `max(min_VCC, min_RPE1, min_K562) = max(10, 25, 27)`, derived
  mechanically from the three observed minima. **All K562-essential constructs qualify.
  VCC constructs with < 27 cells are dropped, and so are RPE1 constructs with 25–26
  cells.** Report exact retained and dropped counts for **every** screen; derive them
  from construct-level counts, never from the recorded minima.

Both are reported. If the two disagree, the result is **sampling-depth dependent** and
must be reported as such — neither is preferred, and neither may be selected after the
fact.

**The two `n` values are not interchangeable, and the reason must be stated before the
numbers exist.** They trade off in opposite directions:

- `n = 10` retains every construct, so it carries **no retention-induced selection**, but
  10 cells per construct leaves substantial residual estimation noise.
- `n = 27` samples more cells and so leaves less residual noise, but **retention itself
  selects on cell count.** VCC's median is 26, so more than half of VCC drops, and the
  survivors are its *high*-cell-count constructs — which, by the within-screen
  association (Spearman −0.72), are systematically the *smaller*-magnitude ones. So VCC's
  matched median at `n = 27` will be pulled down by which constructs survive, not only by
  the matching.

This retention rule **could instantiate** the viability mechanism described in §3: if
low-cell-count constructs also tend to have larger true effects, retaining only
high-cell-count constructs would preferentially remove such genes. **This is a
pre-specified concern, not an established causal explanation.**

Reporting both `n` values is therefore load-bearing, not decorative — a conclusion that
holds only at one of them is a conclusion about that design choice, and must be reported
that way.

**Three pre-registered sampling seeds:** `20270201`, `20270202`, `20270203`, continuing
the existing control-seed scheme and kept distinct from head-training seeds {0,1,2}.
Reporting across seeds shows sensitivity to the draw.

**Retention per screen must be reported for each `n`.** VCC's median is 26, so `n = 27`
will drop roughly half of it. That loss is visible, not hidden.

### 2.1 What is resampled — the same cells for both representations

For each construct and each draw, sample `n_match` cell IDs **once**, and rebuild from
**that same cell set**:

- `x_cell` — the per-construct mean over the sampled **perturbed** cells, minus the NT
  baseline;
- `m̃` — the four magnitude scalars over that `x_cell`, z-scored on the frozen VCC-train
  statistics (§6B);
- `z` — `mean(E over the sampled perturbed cells) − mu_NT`, re-aggregated from the
  **already-persisted per-cell embeddings**.

**Only the perturbed construct is subsampled. The NT reference is not.** Each screen
keeps its already-validated NT convention unchanged — VCC batch-local, RPE1 and
K562-essential their established global pools (11,485 and 10,691 cells). Subsampling the
NT reference as well would change two noise sources at once, and because the NT mean
enters every construct in a screen, it would inject a common offset into precisely the
cross-screen quantity being measured. The sensitivity varies **one factor**: how many
cells estimate the construct's own mean.

Sampling `m̃` while leaving `z` at all-cells would break exactly the construction
parallelism `x_cell` exists to preserve. **No new embedding is computed** — per-cell
embeddings exist for all three screens (Phase 3 for K562-essential, Phase 2 for RPE1,
[FEATURE-PIPELINE]'s read-only cache for VCC); this is re-aggregation only.

**If per-cell embeddings turn out to be unavailable or incomplete for any screen, the
sensitivity for that screen is explicitly magnitude-side only**, and no
construction-matched FM comparison may be claimed from it. State this per screen rather
than assuming coverage.

### 2.2 Scope — descriptive, and bounded

This sensitivity **characterises the feature distributions**. It compares the four
magnitude components, and `z`'s distributional properties, across the three screens at
matched cell counts.

It is **not** a re-run of Phase 4. Whether a matched-feature *evaluation* is warranted is
a separate question requiring its own amendment, and that amendment would have to be
committed **before external `y` is opened**, not after. It is deliberately out of scope
here so that this amendment stays bounded and the *exactly one new result*
constraint remains untouched.

### 2.3 Interpretation, fixed before the numbers exist

- Cross-screen magnitude ordering **persists** at both `n` values and all three seeds →
  the ordering is robust to sampling depth over the range tested.
- Ordering **changes** at either `n` or across seeds → the cross-screen magnitude
  comparison is sampling-depth dependent, and the manuscript must disclose that its
  magnitude features, unlike its endpoint, are not size-matched across screens.

Neither outcome licenses a claim about the feature–outcome relationship. That is §4.

## 3. A VCC-side mechanism diagnostic, authorised now

VCC's `y` is already fully spent: Phase 1 is complete and its single test evaluation is
closed. Therefore, **on VCC train and validation only** — no new statistic on VCC test —
the following may be computed now:

- `corr(n_cells, y)`;
- `corr(n_cells, m̃)` per component;
- the `m̃ ↔ y` association **stratified by** `n_cells` quintile, to see how much survives
  conditioning.

**Motivating hypothesis, labelled as such.** Knocking down an essential gene may reduce
viability → fewer cells recovered → *and* that gene has a larger true effect. Low `n`
would then coincide with large true effect, while also inflating measured magnitude —
producing a partly spurious magnitude↔effect association.

**What this diagnostic can and cannot establish.** It is a **mechanism check on VCC**. A
strong association there makes the viability/sample-size concern more credible; a weak
one makes it less so. **Neither transfers to the external screens** — K562-essential is a
different cell type, a different screen, and by construction an essential-gene panel
where the mechanism is most plausible. Do not generalise the VCC result to them.

## 4. The external confound diagnostic is DEFERRED, and the ordering is binding

`corr(n_cells, y)` and any `m̃ ↔ y` association on **RPE1 or K562-essential** may be
computed **only after** Phase 4's primary locked results have been computed and durably
persisted.

This ordering is the point. Computing a magnitude↔outcome association on an external
screen before the locked evaluation would be looking at the signal Phase 4 exists to
measure. The pre-registration's value is that external `y` was untouched until the locked
moment, and that claim must remain literally true.

**Binding sequence:**

1. A3 committed (this document).
2. Outcome-blind matched artifacts built; VCC train/validation diagnostic run.
3. **Phase 4 primary locked evaluation.** External `y` opened here for the first time.
   Primary results persisted durably before anything else.
4. Pre-registered secondaries (A1's `x_release`, A2's linear head).
5. Only then: `n_cells ↔ external y`, and the interpretive confound diagnostics.

## 5. A correction to my own reasoning, recorded

I argued that the RPE1-versus-VCC magnitude ordering is "conservative" because RPE1 has
more cells and therefore less noise inflation. **That argument rests on an assumption I
did not state: that per-cell noise `σ²` is the same across screens.** Three screens with
different cell types, sequencing runs, and depths give no reason to assume it. If `σ²`
differs, the inflation ordering does not follow from `n` alone and the conclusion can
reverse.

What is established is narrower: **estimation-noise inflation is present within every
screen.** Its contribution to the between-screen ordering is unresolved, and §2 is the
test.
